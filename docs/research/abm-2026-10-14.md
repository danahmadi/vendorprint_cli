# 6sense, Demandbase, and Engagio — DNS feasibility pilot

**Decision: no production signatures added.** A documentation review and exploratory DNS pilot did not establish a vendor-specific pattern suitable for vendorprint. This is a bounded negative result, not proof that a signal cannot exist or that the sampled companies do not use these products.

## Evidence and scope

| Vendor | Public references checked | Result |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 6sense | 10 | No attributable DNS marker found. AI Email requires DNS configuration, but shared SendGrid infrastructure alone cannot establish 6sense usage. |
| Demandbase | 9 | No attributable DNS marker found. The documented website integration uses a JavaScript tag and identification API. |
| Engagio | 7 historical customers | No attributable legacy DNS marker found. Acquisition-era customer references do not establish present-day adoption. |

The [source-linked cohort](abm/cohort.json) was selected before DNS collection. These are vendor-reported relationships, not independently confirmed current contracts. The 6sense references concern the broader product and do not establish that the companies bought AI Email. This is a smaller exploratory pilot than the earlier meeting-intelligence study, with no holdout or known-negative control group.

### 6sense

[6sense WebTag 2.0](https://support.6sense.com/v1/docs/set-up-6sense-webtag-20) is a website integration. A browser requesting a vendor's script or API does not, by itself, create a record in the customer's DNS zone.

[6sense Email Provider for AI Email](https://support.6sense.com/docs/set-up-the-6sense-email-provider-for-aie) is the most promising DNS avenue. Its documentation describes SendGrid-based delivery and generated CNAME/MX authentication records on a secondary domain or subdomain. The public setup screenshot hides the actual host and target values, so it does not establish a reusable 6sense-specific suffix. Generic SendGrid SPF/CNAME/DKIM records must remain SendGrid findings.

[6sense organization subdomains](https://support.6sense.com/docs/configure-organization-name-and-subdomain) identify instances hosted under the vendor's namespace. A suggestive hostname is not evidence linking a customer's domain to that instance. We did not enumerate or probe account/login endpoints.

### Demandbase and legacy Engagio

[Demandbase's tag documentation](https://support.demandbase.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005051743-Understanding-the-Demandbase-Tag) describes a JavaScript integration calling its identification API. [Troubleshooting instructions](https://support.demandbase.com/hc/en-us/articles/360056507252-Troubleshoot-Demandbase-Tag) inspect page source and browser network requests. Those are not customer DNS records, and vendorprint continues to use DNS only.

[Demandbase acquired Engagio on June 16, 2020](https://www.demandbase.com/press-release/demandbase-acquires-engagio/). That announcement supplies the seven historical Engagio customer references below. Any future verified Engagio pattern should explicitly identify legacy Engagio infrastructure associated with Demandbase; it should not automatically assert an active modern Demandbase subscription. The unrelated `engagio.social` or similarly named businesses are not evidence for this product.

## DNS method and limitations

- 26 distinct domains, **2,014 baseline DNS queries**, followed by **26 supplemental apex TXT queries** that explicitly handle verification-key names beginning with a digit, such as `6sense`.
- Baseline: apex TXT and NS; MX at the apex, `email`, and `inbox`; 57 fixed CNAME labels plus a wildcard control; up to ten recursive SPF references and short CNAME chains. Maximum 80 baseline queries per domain, four domains concurrently, at least 30 ms between starts per process.
- Seven domains reached the 80-query cap: DISCO, Pendo, PTC, Tenable, Tipalti, Unanet, and Vonage. Follow-up exploration is therefore incomplete at the cap. Only fixed labels were checked; unknown secondary sending domains, private DNS, arbitrary subdomain labels, CT, and DKIM selector discovery were outside this pilot.
- Three non-absence errors: MX for `inbox.pageuppeople.com` returned SERVFAIL; a downstream CNAME query for `9unyb.x.incapdns.net` also returned SERVFAIL; a downstream CNAME query for `wac.1df5.edgecastcdn.net` timed out while following a Trend Micro chain. These are retained in the results and are not interpreted as evidence of absence.
- No raw TXT verification values were retained. Persisted observations contain prefixes, host references, record counts, public CNAME/MX/NS answers, and statuses. The committed [results](abm/results.json) summarize query health and the absence of attributable candidate hits; full redacted observations remain in the local research workspace.
- No customer-page execution, cookies, mailbox access, SMTP probing, account-endpoint probing, or private customer data. Reading official documentation and published customer references was research, not runtime website fingerprinting.

We also searched the earlier meeting-intelligence observations for these vendor names. Demandbase's own Marketo, Zendesk, Skilljar, and DMARC-provider records describe Demandbase's stack; they do not identify companies using Demandbase.

Observation timestamps fall on October 15 UTC / October 14 America/Los_Angeles. There is no estimated precision or recall, and no new supported-vendor claim for any of these three vendors.

## What would justify a signature

A documented product-specific DNS target or verification prefix, corroborated on a known customer domain, could support a narrowly scoped detector. For 6sense AI Email, the missing evidence is the actual vendor-specific MX/CNAME namespace, if one exists, rather than generic SendGrid records. Repeated independently attributable legacy targets could support a qualified Engagio rule. Neither requirement was satisfied by this pilot.

To repeat the bounded baseline and supplementary prefix check with Node 20 or newer:

```sh
node docs/research/abm/probe.mjs
node docs/research/abm/check-prefixes.mjs
```

These commands make live DNS queries and overwrite their respective local research outputs. DNS changes over time. The research files are excluded from the npm tarball.
